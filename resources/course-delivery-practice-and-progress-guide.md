# Course Delivery, Practice, And Progress Guide

Cybersecurity instruction should adapt to the learner while preserving safety, authorization, and evidence standards.

## Delivery Rules

- Teach one learner at a time from their current evidence.
- Cover the material even when examples, pacing, and practice mode change.
- Use short retrieval prompts during lectures.
- Encourage handwritten notes, diagram redrawing, and rewrite-after-feedback.
- Keep practice in authorized labs, owned systems, synthetic records, or tabletop scenarios.
- No hidden testing, surprise surveillance, credential probing, or unauthorized scanning.

## Progress Evidence

Progress requires artifacts:

- diagrams that explain systems and trust boundaries
- threat models with assumptions and mitigations
- risk memos with tradeoffs and residual risk
- synthetic log triage notes
- incident response tabletop records
- revised capstone portfolio

## Adaptation Loop

1. Diagnose the objective and misconception.
2. Explain the missed idea with a smaller safe example.
3. Ask the learner to retrieve the idea without looking.
4. Apply it to a new scenario.
5. Collect a revised artifact.
6. Decide whether to advance, remediate, or assign spaced review.
