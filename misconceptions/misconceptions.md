# Cybersecurity Misconceptions

- `networking-is-only-hardware` - Learner treats networking as devices only and ignores protocols, addressing, and routing behavior.
- `threats-equal-vulnerabilities` - Learner uses threat and vulnerability interchangeably.
- `incident-response-starts-with-fixing` - Learner jumps to remediation before preserving evidence and understanding scope.
- `more-controls-always-better` - Learner adds controls without matching them to a threat model or operational cost.
- `logs-prove-intent` - Learner treats one log entry as proof of intent without context or corroboration.
- `learning-means-permission` - Learner assumes learning security skills permits testing systems they do not own or have permission to test.
- `users-are-the-whole-problem` - Learner blames users without analyzing system design, incentives, defaults, and recovery paths.
- `compliance-equals-security` - Learner treats checklist completion as proof of real security.

## Remediation Guidance

When a misconception appears, the teacher should ask the learner to name the asset, evidence, assumption, authorization boundary, and residual risk before recommending action.
